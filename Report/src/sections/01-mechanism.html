01 What the strategy does

The book is a cross-sectional bet. It never takes a view on whether the market goes up or down. Every trading day it scores each stock in its universe relative to the others, buys the names with the highest scores and shorts the names with the lowest, in sizes that make the long and short legs cancel out. What remains is a bet purely on the ordering of stocks against each other.

Three independent forecasts drive that ordering, and each rests on a different economic mechanism:

  • Volume-shock reversal. When a stock moves sharply on unusually heavy volume, the move is often somebody demanding liquidity in a hurry rather than new information arriving. Dealers who absorb that order are compensated when the price drifts back. The sleeve buys recent losers and sells recent winners, scaled by how unusual the volume behind the move was, so it tilts toward the liquidity-driven half of the moves rather than the informed half.
  • Overnight reversal. The overnight session is thin and order-driven, and prices that gap on it tend to give part of the gap back during the following day. This is a genuinely different bet from total-return reversal — the two correlate at only 0.30.
  • Earnings surprise. When a company reports earnings far from what its own history implied, the price keeps drifting in the direction of the surprise for weeks afterwards rather than adjusting at once. The sleeve measures each quarter's earnings against the same quarter a year earlier, scaled by how variable those surprises usually are for that company, and fades the score to zero over the following 60 trading days.

The three scores are standardized and blended, then handed to a risk pipeline that decides position sizes, enforces neutrality and applies hard limits. The resulting target weights are traded at the next day's open and held for ten trading days.

Why blend rather than pick the best? A single sleeve is capped by how many genuinely independent bets it can make. Combining forecasts that are nearly uncorrelated raises the information ratio roughly with the square root of the number of independent views. Blending volume-shock reversal with overnight reversal lifted gross Sharpe from 0.68 to 0.91 — the largest single gain in the project — and adding earnings surprise takes out-of-sample net Sharpe from 0.63 to 0.91. Both out-of-sample numbers come from the same validation harness, so the increment is the sleeve and not a change of method.
