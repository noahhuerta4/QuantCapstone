12 Limitations

Every item here makes the true result worse than the reported one. None is hidden in an appendix because each changes how the numbers should be read.

Survivorship bias. The universe is seeded from current S&P 500 membership, and yfinance serves only currently-listed tickers, so companies that fell far enough to be delisted are absent from the entire history. For a reversal strategy the direction of this bias is unambiguous: the missing names are disproportionately those whose "oversold, buy it" signal never reverted. The reported results should be read as an upper bound. Fixing it properly needs a point-in-time constituent source or a delisting-inclusive history.

  • Costs not modelled. Margin financing on the long leg beyond cash, which a book running 1.0× gross genuinely pays. Hard-to-borrow rates above general collateral — and a reversal signal most wants to short recent winners, which skew expensive to borrow, so the uniform rate is a floor. Fill uncertainty: every intended trade is assumed to execute in full at the modelled price.
  • The spread is calibrated, not measured. Without quote data or real fills, the half-spread comes from a function fitted to published levels. The impact coefficient is likewise assumed. This is why the break-even multiple of 6.43× carries more weight than the base case.
  • Ten years is one sample. 2015–2024 was a specific regime. The minimum track record length needed to call this Sharpe significantly positive is 802 trading days and we have 1,638 — comfortable, but not by an order of magnitude.
  • The sector map is a current snapshot. Companies change GICS classification; sector neutrality is imposed using today's labels applied backwards.
  • Backtested performance is not live performance. Which is precisely why the cost model and the overfitting controls carry most of the weight here, and why the out-of-sample number with its confidence interval is the only claim being made.
