The drawdown panel is the honest half of the chart. The worst decline is only 5.1%, but it took 438 trading days — most of two years — to recover, and the book spends long stretches underwater. A rising line hides that; an investor living through 2022 would not have experienced it as a steady climb.

Capital size is a real constraint, in both directions. A small account cannot run this strategy at all: at $10,000 with a hundred names the positions would be about $100 each, no trade would clear the $200 minimum size, and the book would never trade. The fixed per-order commission is what bites on the way down — net Sharpe is 0.95 at $1m and 0.89 at $100,000, where commission has risen from 14 to 38 bps a year.

Above the sweet spot the binding constraint changes from fixed costs to market impact. $1m scores better than $10m (0.954 against 0.938) because it consumes a tenth as much of each name's daily volume — 0.004% against 0.036% — so it pays less impact. The strategy has a capacity range, not a monotone preference for size.
