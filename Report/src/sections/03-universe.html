03 Universe and data

The tradable universe is the 100 most liquid US large-cap common stocks, selected each month by trailing 63-day average dollar volume from current S&P 500 membership. Membership is held fixed between monthly refreshes: re-ranking daily would churn the book at the universe boundary for no forecasting reason.

Selection uses only information available before the day being selected for. Average dollar volume is lagged one bar, eligibility screens use lagged prices, and names must have at least 252 bars of history and trade above $5.

The filing date is what makes fundamentals honest. A company's December-quarter figures have a period end of 31 December but are not public until the report is filed in February. Ranking stocks on them in January would be trading on information nobody had. The common workaround is to lag all accounting data by six months, which is safe but discards months of genuinely available information. Because the SEC stamps every fact with its filing date, a value here becomes visible on the exact day it was filed and not before. Restatements handle themselves: a company that refiles 2019 numbers in 2021 produces a second fact with a later filing date, so a 2019 query still returns what the market actually had in 2019.
